# DESCENT — Story

The narrative content of the experience. Read this if you want to know *what is said*, not *how it works*.

A first-person account from someone who has never seen the surface, set on a world a thousand years after the planet froze, in a deep-sea station beneath a tightening band of liveable water.

---

## Before · the process

Before the experience itself, ten design axes appear in faded white on black. One by one — one per second — our world's choices light up:

> **+1000 years · matriarchal · waterworld · silent · almost no sunlight · low-tech · 0°C · africa · blue · dystopian**

The frame holds for a beat, fades to black, and the title appears.

## Title

> *a world beneath ice. a station still breathing.*
> *an eye watching below.*
>
> *find the signals. listen before you ascend.*
>
> **world 3324255146 · year 3126**
>
> click to descend · use headphones

A click begins the experience. A low drone bridges from this moment into the first scene. The first words rise as subtitle:

> *you wake. the air is heavy. wet.*
> *you have never seen the surface.*

## Scene 1 · the world

Dark teal. The sea. Particles drift. Whales pass. Sea devils glide between cages with bioluminescent lures pulsing on a 4.7-second heartbeat. The cursor disturbs the water; a deep filter responds to vertical movement. Three signals pulse, scattered.

If left alone too long, a whisper:
*awake.* · *remember.* · *ascend.* · *listen.*

### shrinking belt
> the sun is lethal within hours.
> the ice keeps expanding from both poles.
> only a narrow belt remains —
> held open by heat from below.
> we've been down here for over a thousand years.

A Mollweide world: four continents in dim green. Polar ice caps creeping inward as the year ticks from 2026 to 3126. Geothermal vents glow along the equator. A sun above with lethal radiation rays.

### deep parade
> the colder the water, the more oxygen it holds.
> the more oxygen, the larger they grow.
> we are no longer the largest thing in the ocean.

A parade of vast deep-sea creatures crosses left to right. A tiny human silhouette stands at the bottom for scale.

### sea level
> the seven continents no longer exist.
> only the peaks of former mountain ranges
> rise above the surface — like bones.

Mountain silhouettes against a burnt sun-bleached sky. Water rises across the screen as the year counts up. By the end the mountains are islands.

After all three signals have been heard, **ascend** appears.

> *we ascend.*
> *into the station.*

---

## Scene 2 · the station

Red emergency light. The room is mostly black; light comes from a ceiling lamp and a faint torch where the cursor passes. As the player enters, a single soft tap on the hull:

> *— a hand on the wall, on waking.*

After a moment, a whisper from somewhere inside the station:

> *the walls answer when struck.*

Thirty hull pieces are scattered through the room, each one tappable. The first one struck whispers back:

> *the workers know this language.*

Three or more taps within ten seconds, and a ring of resonance grows around the last struck piece. Seven taps in ten seconds, and a cascading wave runs from top to bottom of the room — every hull piece sounds in sequence. The first time this happens:

> *the station sings back.*

The second time:

> *they're listening below.*

After that:

> *the deep answers.*

A subtitle has set the room: *the station. systems humming. walls pressing close.* In presentation mode it is replaced with:
*module 7656. the deeps. this is where i sleep. eat. breathe.*

Four signals pulse in the room.

### hierarchy
> proximity to light determines everything.
> those above decide. those below maintain.
> those at the bottom are forgotten.

A vertical cross-section of the station. Upper level: filtered sunlight, the council of mothers. Middle level: workers, technicians, farmers. The deeps: maintenance, outcasts, darkness. *Proximity to light determines social standing.*

### daily life
*— the room around the viz dims; trickling water and distant kitchen sounds wash in*

> we speak in borrowed words and water sounds.
> you eat what the ocean gives.
> you sleep when the machines quiet.
> you strike the walls — and call it singing.

A daily cycle as a clock: rest cycle · first interval · work shift · communal meal · hull songs · silence hours. *No sun. No day. No night. Life follows the rhythm of machines.*

### air / oxygen
> one of the lower modules lost pressure again.
> our systems are old — some built before the great flood.
> we keep them running with patches and prayer.

An air system schematic — pressure gauges, valves, warning lights.

### the mothers · n°39
> they count their years from the surface.
> the oldest was born to someone who remembered sky.
> we call them the mothers.
> they have not been asked our names.
> the watcher mother decides who walks up.

Five mothers — provisioner, engineer, memory, navigator, watcher — each with her glyph and her role. The watcher chooses who climbs to the altar.

### offering to kheir
> the third week. the first blood.
> a cloth, weighted, set to drift upward.
> kheir keeps what he chooses to keep.
> the mothers count permits.
> the women count tides.

A vertical water-cut. The station crouches at the bottom of the frame, dark. The ice ceiling above; the moon over the ice, halo soft. From a tiny warm pinprick of light on the station's upper chamber, a single cloth — weighted at the hem — drifts slowly upward, breathing on the 4.7-second heartbeat. Earlier offerings hang faded at intermediate heights, previous cycles still on their way to Kheir or already kept by him.

After all four signals: **ascend.**

---

## Silence hours

Between the station and the eye: a ritual quiet. The station audio fades out. For almost three seconds, only a barely-audible underwater bed and, somewhere in it, a single long metal creak. The eye scene appears through the dark before the new soundscape rises.

This is the quiet the station keeps each cycle. A held breath. *No bug.*

---

## Scene 3 · the eye

> *the chamber. the altar.*
> *the eye.*

A vertical light beam falls from above. Where it lands on the floor, faint concentric rings breathe on the 4.7-second heartbeat. Dust drifts in the beam. Embers rise.

If the player lingers in the chamber, after fifteen seconds a single whisper arrives:
> *those who return pause for 4.7 seconds.*
> *before they answer.*

The heartbeat names itself.

Two signals.

### mythology
> vahra. the sun goddess. she turned away.
> kheir. the moon. he holds the ice. he mourns.
> we built the eye to look back at her.
> if she accepts you — you see.
> if not — you never see again.

### selection
> they say my name. once.
> that's enough.
> something tightens inside me.

Then, one line at a time, every second or so:

> i walk · too fast then slower · i could stop · but i keep walking ·
> the path narrows · the air thins · the chamber is smaller than i expected ·
> the altar stands in the center · the eye · two tubes · they lean toward me ·
> too close · i kneel · the floor is cold · i am not the first ·
> the light is not mine · i raise my gaze · slowly · toward the openings

After both signals: **ascend** — the final one.

## The climax · the look

The eye comes down.

### Phase A — *the watcher mother*
The beam brightens. Golden particles dance. A subtitle, sand-coloured:

> *the watcher mother has chosen you.*
> *she has seen how you [verb].*
> *she calls you — [label].*

The verb and label come from what the player did. *(See "what she sees" below.)*

After about a second and a half:

> *the tubes adjust*
> *now*
> *i raise my gaze*
> *slowly*

### Phase B — *the look*
A circle is drawn around the tube opening. For 4.7 seconds — one heartbeat — the player chooses what to do with their gaze.

Cursor inside the circle:
> *i raise my gaze*
> *slowly*

Cursor outside:
> *do not look directly at it*

A bar fills around the tube. At the end of the heartbeat, the eye decides based on how long the player held the gaze.

### Phase C — *the verdict*

Three possible outcomes.

**Looked** *(held gaze most of the time)*
A blinding white wash floods the screen, then black. A single word arrives, warm and small:

> *light.*

A faint warmth lingers in the world after this. The seen-light remains.

**Undecided** *(held briefly)*
> *i didn't decide.*

**Looked away** *(barely or never inside the circle)*
> *i looked away.*

---

## What she sees

The watcher mother names the player based on how they spent their time in the station. The label is the second-person verdict; the verb is what they did to earn it.

Currently the only label that triggers is for those who struck the walls repeatedly:

> **the noise-maker** — *you strike the walls*

More labels are open for the future — for those who held still, for those who freed sea devils, for those who lingered in particular hotspots, for those who never quite looked at the eye.

---

## The shape of the thing

A line falls through all of it: *you have never seen the surface* at the start, *light* at the end. Someone who has only ever known the deep, who learned to read the wall, who held still in front of an altar, who looked up.

The 4.7-second heartbeat runs underneath every scene. Cages breathe on it. The altar rings breathe on it. The final look is timed to it. The world has a pulse, and the player decides whether to look into it.
